Backtest summary · 29 Oct 2026 · corrected figures

NIFTY Iron Condor Playbook

The rule set chosen for the forward test of a monthly NIFTY iron condor, and the backtest behind it: 68 monthly cycles from January 2021 to August 2026 (61 traded, 5 skipped by the rules, 2 without usable data), replayed on 1-minute option prices from ICICI Breeze and checked against StockMock.

1 lot per tradeP&L after costs (≈₹250 per condor)No slippageOne condor open at a time

These are backtest results for paper-trading decisions, not investment advice.

The short version

Trade only calm months, and widen the hedge to 400 points when the premium is rich

The forward-test rule made ₹90,667 on 37 trades in the backtest, against ₹69,855 on 45 trades for the earlier rule, with a smaller drawdown. With a 20-delta put credit spread in the months the condor skips, the whole rulebook made ₹1,03,880 on 61 trades with the same −₹5,523 drawdown. A 600-point version earned more (₹1,22,477), but its worst case per trade breaks the limit of 2–3% of capital, so it is set aside for a review after 6–12 months. The thresholds were chosen on this same data, so treat the figures as an upper bound.

Forward test · from October 2026

The rule being paper traded

Risk and reward

600 points makes more profit; 400 points is chosen for its lower risk

The two versions trade the same 37 months and differ only in the 18 rich-credit months. The 600-point hedge gives the better profit, ₹1,22,477 against ₹90,667. The 400-point hedge is still the one being traded, because the 600-point condor can lose about ₹22,500 a lot on a single bad month, more than the 2–3% of capital limit allows on a ₹3–5L account, and per rupee at risk it is no better.

Added 27 Oct 2026 · months without a condor

In the skipped months, a 20-delta put credit spread with a 200-point hedge

When the condor rule skips a month, sell the ~20-delta put and buy the put 200 points lower, with the same entry, 15:16 check and 18-DTE exit. Target 50% and stop 100% of the spread's own credit. One exception: after a 10-day rise above 2.5% whose last five days added +1% or less, the rally has stalled, and the month is skipped. A 300-point hedge makes more, but for the same reason as 400 against 600 it is only shadow-logged and will be reviewed together with the condor width.

Before real money

Risks and limits

  • A bad rich-credit month has not been seen yet. A 400-point condor can lose about ₹13,600 a lot; no rich-credit month in the backtest lost more than about ₹1,600.
  • Thresholds fitted on this data. Four numbers (2%, 45%, 40%, 60% target) were chosen after seeing 2021–26, and several results hang on one trade.
  • Few trades. About six condors a year, 37 in total. Close to half the condor profit (43%) came from 2025.
  • A harder period exists. In 2019–20 the earlier rule had a drawdown of about ₹19,000 a lot.
  • Slippage and margin. Each condor has 8 order legs and each put spread 4; 1 point per leg takes about ₹22,000 off the six-year rulebook total. Check margin in the broker's calculator.
  • Modest returns on capital. About ₹18,000 a year for the whole rulebook in the backtest is 3.5–6% a year on ₹3–5L. More lots or a wider hedge scale profit, drawdown and the worst case together.

  5. Decide in advance what would stop the test, for example a drawdown beyond about ₹13,000 a lot (the reshuffled-trade estimate) or average slippage above 1 point per leg.
  6. Review the hedge width after 6–12 months (around April–October 2027): 400 against 600 points for the condor and 200 against 300 points for the put spread, with real fills and margin.
