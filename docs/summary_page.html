Backtest summary · 28 Oct 2026

NIFTY Iron Condor Playbook

The rule set chosen for the forward test of a monthly NIFTY iron condor, and the backtest behind it: 66 monthly trades from January 2021 to August 2026, replayed on 1-minute option prices from ICICI Breeze and checked against StockMock.

1 lot per tradeP&L after costs (≈₹250 per condor)No slippageOne condor open at a time

These are backtest results for paper-trading decisions, not investment advice.

The short version

Trade only calm months, and widen the hedge to 400 points when the premium is rich

The forward-test rule made ₹85,729 on 36 trades in the backtest, against ₹66,052 on 45 trades for the earlier rule, with a smaller drawdown. With a 20-delta put credit spread in the months the condor skips, the whole rulebook made ₹97,843 on 61 trades with the same −₹5,523 drawdown. A 600-point version earned more (₹114,939), but its worst case per trade breaks the limit of 2–3% of capital, so it is set aside for a review after 6–12 months. The thresholds were chosen on this same data, so treat the figures as an upper bound.

Forward test · from October 2026

The rule being paper traded

Two readings decide each month. The evening before entry: NIFTY's 10-day move, from NSE's official closes. At 11:16 on the entry day: the net credit of a 400-point condor (sold ~30-delta call and put, hedges 400 points out). That same condor is the trade when its credit is rich.

Risk and reward

600 points makes more profit; 400 points is chosen for its lower risk

The two versions trade the same 36 months and differ only in the 17 rich-credit months. The 600-point hedge gives the better profit, ₹114,939 against ₹85,729. The 400-point hedge is still the one being traded, because the 600-point condor can lose about ₹22,500 a lot on a single bad month, more than the 2–3% of capital limit allows on a ₹3–5L account, and per rupee at risk it is no better.

Added 27 Oct 2026 · months without a condor

In the skipped months, a 20-delta put credit spread with a 200-point hedge

When the condor rule skips a month, sell the ~20-delta put and buy the put 200 points lower, with the same entry, 15:16 check and 18-DTE exit. Target 50% and stop 100% of the spread's own credit. One exception: after a 10-day rise above 2.5% whose last five days added +1% or less, the rally has stalled, and the month is skipped. A 300-point hedge makes more, but for the same reason as 400 against 600 it is only shadow-logged and will be reviewed together with the condor width.

Before real money

Risks and limits

  • A bad rich-credit month has not been seen yet. A 400-point condor can lose about ₹13,600 a lot; no rich-credit month in the backtest lost more than about ₹1,600.
  • Thresholds fitted on this data. Four numbers (2%, 45%, 40%, 60% target) were chosen after seeing 2021–26, and several results hang on one trade.
  • Few trades. About six a year, 36 in total. Close to half the profit came from 2025.
  • A harder period exists. In 2019–20 the earlier rule had a drawdown of about ₹19,000 a lot.
  • Slippage and margin. Each condor has 8 order legs; 1 point per leg takes about ₹16,000 off the six-year total. Check margin in the broker's calculator.
  • Modest returns on capital. About ₹15,000 a year in the backtest is 3–5% a year on ₹3–5L. More lots or a wider hedge scale profit, drawdown and the worst case together.
Action items

Next steps

  1. Forward test with 1 lot using the rules above: the condor, or the 20-delta / 200-point put spread in a month without one. The next decision is on Monday 16 November 2026, for the 29 December expiry.
  2. Shadow-log the earlier rule every month, including this October's 300-point condor (time exit 5 November 2026).
  3. Keep a journal: bid, ask and fill of every leg at entry and exit, margin blocked, the 400-point credit, and the exit reason.
  4. Flag borderline readings: a 10-day move within 0.1 of ±2%, or a credit within a few points of 160 or 180.
  5. Decide in advance what would stop the test, for example a drawdown beyond about ₹13,000 a lot (the reshuffled-trade estimate) or average slippage above 1 point per leg.
  6. Review the hedge width after 6–12 months (around April–October 2027): 400 against 600 points for the condor and 200 against 300 points for the put spread, with real fills and margin.
